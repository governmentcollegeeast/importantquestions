What are the Provisions regarding Leave with Wages under the Factories Act, 1948?
Government College Ludhiana East • Commercial Law — B.Com (Sem II) Prepared by: Jeevansh Manocha

Introduction

Workers employed in factories continuously perform physical and mental labour under demanding industrial conditions. In order to protect their health, improve efficiency, and provide necessary rest and recreation, the Factories Act, 1948 contains important provisions regarding leave with wages.

These provisions ensure that workers receive paid leave after working for a specified period. The objective is to promote worker welfare, maintain health standards, and improve industrial efficiency.

Meaning of Leave with Wages

Leave with wages means a period of authorized absence from work during which the worker is entitled to receive wages. It allows workers to take rest while still receiving financial benefits from the employer.

Provisions regarding Leave with Wages under the Factories Act, 1948

The important provisions relating to leave with wages are as follows:

  • 1. Eligibility for Leave: A worker who has worked for at least 240 days or more in a factory during a calendar year becomes eligible for annual leave with wages.
  • 2. Rate of Leave for Adult Workers: An adult worker is entitled to one day leave for every twenty days of work performed during the previous calendar year.
  • 3. Rate of Leave for Child Workers: A child worker is entitled to one day leave for every fifteen days of work performed.
  • 4. Accumulation of Leave: Unused leave may be carried forward to the next year subject to the prescribed limit under the Act.
  • 5. Wages during Leave Period: Workers are entitled to receive wages during the leave period at the rate of daily average of their total earnings.
  • 6. Advance Payment for Leave: Where leave is taken for a certain number of days, wages for the leave period should be paid in advance before the leave begins.
  • 7. Leave Book: The employer must maintain proper records and leave books relating to leave granted to workers.
  • 8. Application for Leave: Workers are generally required to apply for leave according to prescribed procedures and rules of the factory.
  • 9. Payment in case of Discharge or Dismissal: If a worker is discharged, dismissed, or leaves employment, wages for earned leave not availed must be paid.

Objectives of Leave with Wages

  • To provide rest and relaxation to workers.
  • To protect physical and mental health of employees.
  • To improve efficiency and productivity.
  • To promote worker welfare and satisfaction.
  • To reduce fatigue and industrial accidents.

Importance of Leave with Wages

Leave with wages is an important welfare measure for workers. Continuous work without proper rest may negatively affect the health and efficiency of employees. Paid leave helps workers maintain physical fitness and mental well-being.

It also improves morale, reduces stress, and creates better employer-employee relations. Therefore, leave provisions contribute significantly to industrial harmony and productivity.

Critical Analysis

The provisions regarding leave with wages under the Factories Act, 1948 reflect the welfare-oriented approach of labour legislation in India. These provisions recognize that workers are human beings who require adequate rest and recreation for maintaining efficiency and health.

However, in some industries workers are unable to fully enjoy these benefits due to workload pressures and lack of awareness regarding labour rights. Therefore, strict implementation and awareness among workers are essential.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the provisions regarding leave with wages under the Factories Act, 1948 play an important role in promoting worker welfare and maintaining healthy working conditions in factories.

These provisions ensure that workers receive proper rest along with financial security during leave periods. Therefore, leave with wages is an essential welfare measure that contributes to worker satisfaction, industrial peace, and increased productivity.
