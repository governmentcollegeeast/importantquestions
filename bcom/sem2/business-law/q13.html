Discuss the Provisions of the Factories Act, 1948 regarding Health and Welfare of Workers
Government College Ludhiana East • Commercial Law — B.Com (Sem II) Prepared by: Jeevansh Manocha

Introduction

The welfare and protection of workers is one of the primary objectives of labour legislation in India. Industrial workers often work under difficult and risky conditions which may affect their physical and mental health. To ensure proper working conditions and safeguard workers from unhealthy environments, the government enacted the Factories Act, 1948.

The Act contains several provisions regarding health, safety, and welfare of workers employed in factories. These provisions aim at providing hygienic working conditions, welfare facilities, and protection against occupational hazards.

Healthy and satisfied workers contribute significantly towards industrial efficiency, productivity, and industrial peace.

Objectives of the Factories Act, 1948

  • To ensure healthy working conditions in factories.
  • To protect workers from industrial hazards.
  • To provide welfare facilities for employees.
  • To improve efficiency and productivity of labour.
  • To prevent exploitation of workers.

Provisions regarding Health of Workers

The Factories Act provides several important measures for protecting health of workers.

  • 1. Cleanliness: Factories must be kept clean and free from dirt, dust, and bad smell. Floors and walls should be properly cleaned regularly.
  • 2. Disposal of Wastes and Effluents: Proper arrangements should be made for disposal of industrial waste and harmful effluents.
  • 3. Ventilation and Temperature: Factories must provide proper ventilation and maintain suitable temperature for workers.
  • 4. Dust and Fumes: Measures should be taken to prevent inhalation of harmful dust and fumes.
  • 5. Artificial Humidification: Where humidification is artificially increased, prescribed standards must be followed.
  • 6. Overcrowding: Factories should avoid overcrowding to maintain healthy conditions.
  • 7. Lighting: Proper lighting arrangements must be provided in work areas.
  • 8. Drinking Water: Safe and clean drinking water facilities must be provided for workers.
  • 9. Latrines and Urinals: Separate and hygienic sanitary facilities must be provided for male and female workers.
  • 10. Spittoons: Factories should provide proper spittoons and maintain cleanliness around them.

Provisions regarding Welfare of Workers

Apart from health measures, the Act also provides several welfare facilities for workers.

  • 1. Washing Facilities: Suitable washing facilities should be provided separately for male and female workers.
  • 2. Facilities for Storing and Drying Clothes: Workers should be provided facilities for keeping and drying clothes.
  • 3. Sitting Facilities: Workers required to stand during work should be provided suitable seating arrangements.
  • 4. First Aid Appliances: Factories must maintain first aid boxes and medical facilities for treatment of injured workers.
  • 5. Canteens: Factories employing a prescribed number of workers must provide canteen facilities.
  • 6. Shelters and Rest Rooms: Proper rest rooms and shelters should be provided for workers.
  • 7. Creches: Factories employing women workers must provide creche facilities for children.
  • 8. Welfare Officers: Large factories are required to appoint welfare officers for worker welfare activities.

Importance of Health and Welfare Provisions

  • Protect workers from diseases and health hazards.
  • Improve productivity and efficiency.
  • Create healthy working conditions.
  • Reduce accidents and absenteeism.
  • Promote industrial peace and worker satisfaction.

Critical Analysis

The health and welfare provisions under the Factories Act, 1948 represent an important step towards protection of labour rights and social welfare. These provisions recognize that workers are valuable human resources whose well-being is essential for industrial growth.

Although the law provides comprehensive safeguards, proper implementation remains a challenge in certain industries due to poor enforcement and lack of awareness. Therefore, strict compliance and regular inspections are necessary.

In modern industrial society, these provisions continue to play an important role in ensuring safe and humane working conditions for workers.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the Factories Act, 1948 contains important provisions regarding health and welfare of workers to ensure safe, hygienic, and humane working conditions in factories.

These provisions protect workers from unhealthy environments and provide welfare facilities necessary for their physical and mental well-being. Therefore, proper implementation of these provisions is essential for industrial harmony, worker welfare, and economic development.
