Nature, Importance and Principles of Life Insurance
Government College Ludhiana East • Financial Literacy — B.Com Prepared by: Jeevansh Manocha

Introduction

Human life is uncertain and exposed to various risks such as death, illness, accidents, and disability. The sudden death of an earning member can create serious financial difficulties for the family. In such situations, life insurance provides financial security and protection to dependents.

Life insurance has become an essential part of financial planning in modern society. It not only provides protection against uncertainties of life but also promotes savings and investment habits among individuals.

Due to its social and economic importance, life insurance plays a significant role in ensuring financial stability and economic development.

Meaning of Life Insurance

Life insurance is a contract between the insurer and the insured under which the insurer agrees to pay a specified amount of money to the nominee or beneficiary in case of death of the insured person or after the maturity period in exchange for a premium.

It provides financial protection and security to the family of the insured person.

Nature of Life Insurance

The nature and characteristics of life insurance can be understood through the following points:

1. Contractual Agreement

Life insurance is a legal contract between the insurance company and the insured person.

The insurer promises to provide financial compensation according to the terms and conditions of the policy.

2. Protection Against Risk

Life insurance provides financial protection against the risk of death and uncertainty of human life.

It ensures financial security for family members after the death of the earning member.

3. Element of Savings

Life insurance encourages regular savings through periodic premium payments.

Certain life insurance policies also provide maturity benefits and investment returns.

4. Long-Term Contract

Life insurance policies are generally long-term contracts extending over several years.

The insured pays premiums regularly throughout the policy period.

5. Presence of Insurable Interest

The insured person must have an insurable interest in the life insured.

This means there should be a financial relationship or dependency between the insured and beneficiary.

6. Based on Utmost Good Faith

Both parties must disclose all material facts honestly while entering into the insurance contract.

Importance of Life Insurance

Life insurance is important for individuals, families, and the economy in many ways.

1. Financial Security to Family

Life insurance provides financial support to family members after the death of the insured person.

It helps dependents maintain their standard of living.

2. Encourages Savings

Regular payment of premiums develops the habit of disciplined savings among individuals.

3. Investment Opportunity

Certain life insurance policies provide investment benefits and returns along with risk protection.

4. Tax Benefits

Life insurance policies often provide tax benefits on premium payments and maturity amounts according to government laws.

5. Loan Facility

Policyholders can obtain loans against life insurance policies in times of financial need.

6. Contribution to Economic Development

Insurance companies invest collected premiums in industries, infrastructure projects, and government securities.

This promotes capital formation and economic growth.

7. Mental Peace and Security

Life insurance provides confidence and mental peace by reducing fear regarding future financial uncertainties.

Principles of Life Insurance

Life insurance contracts are based on certain important principles which ensure fairness and proper functioning of the insurance system.

1. Principle of Utmost Good Faith

Both insurer and insured must disclose all material facts honestly.

Incorrect or hidden information may make the contract invalid.

2. Principle of Insurable Interest

The insured must have a financial interest in the life insured.

Insurable interest must exist at the time of taking the policy.

3. Principle of Certainty

Death is certain, although the timing of death is uncertain.

Life insurance is based on this certainty of human death.

4. Principle of No Indemnity

Life insurance is not strictly a contract of indemnity because the actual financial loss arising from death cannot be measured exactly.

The insurer pays the fixed amount mentioned in the policy.

5. Principle of Assignment and Nomination

The policyholder may transfer rights of the policy to another person through assignment or nominate a beneficiary to receive policy benefits.

Conclusion

Life insurance is an important financial instrument that provides protection against uncertainties of life and ensures financial security for family members. Its nature includes risk protection, savings, long-term financial planning, and contractual obligations.

Life insurance is highly important because it encourages savings, provides financial stability, supports investment activities, and contributes to economic development. The principles of life insurance such as utmost good faith, insurable interest, and certainty ensure fairness and effective functioning of insurance contracts.

Therefore, life insurance is an essential tool for personal financial security as well as national economic progress.
